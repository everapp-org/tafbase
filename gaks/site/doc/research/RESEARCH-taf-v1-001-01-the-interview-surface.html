What this is#

A coverage map: the corpus's concepts against the questions the market actually asks. Source: a widely-shared "Top 25 Playwright interview questions" list (LinkedIn, 2026 SDET interview prep). The corpus's stated job is to serve as a learning surface and interview prep — this document measures that promise against an external yardstick and names the gaps as work.

What the map says#

Seven covered outright, ~4 partial, 14 gaps. The gaps cluster into themes, each of which is a lane, not just a page:

  • Flakiness & resilience (6, 16): retry policy, quarantine — a real feature lane, not only docs.
  • Element dynamics (8, 9): dropdowns/iframes/uploads — the step language grows (select exists; frame, upload, dialog don't).
  • Auth & state reuse (12, 13): storageState maps cleanly to a model clause — declared once, applied to journeys that need it.
  • Execution control (14, 15, 20, 21): workers, shards, CI matrix, browsers — belongs in a run clause or per-suite config.
  • Observability (10, 19, 22, 23): screenshots on failure, Playwright trace, API mocking — lanes of their own.

The TypeScript question#

The market's assumed Playwright language is TypeScript (the question list presumes it). A third emitter is the right move — and cheap: taf/ops.py already carries the canonical plan, so emit_ts.py renders syntax only, and the parity tests generalize to three suites with the same byte-identical manifest. TypeScript also demos Playwright's native API surface (test()/expect fixtures via test.extend) — arguably the most idiomatic target of the three.

Worth doing, ordered: (a) the flaky/auth/execution gaps are product substance; (b) emit_ts.py is visibility — it makes the parity claim three-legged and lands in the market's assumed idiom. Both are lanes; the concept pages for each land as they're implemented, per ADR-002.
